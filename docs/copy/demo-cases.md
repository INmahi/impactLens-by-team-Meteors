# Demo cases

Owner: science-writer. Three short scripts for the live demo and video.

**All numbers the app shows now are SYNTHETIC.** They were generated with known trends to test the app. They are not measurements. Do not quote them as findings. The "SYNTHETIC DATA" label must stay on screen. Replace the expected results below once real data is loaded.

## 1. Bay of Bengal sea level vs the global mean

Clicks: Variable **Sea level** -> Region **Bay of Bengal (northern)** -> full range -> Compare **Another region** -> **Global** -> **Run analysis**.

Say: "This is satellite sea level averaged over the open northern Bay, about 100 km offshore, not the value at any town. In this synthetic data it rises faster than the global mean, and the test says that difference is very unlikely to be chance. Land sinking in the Ganges-Brahmaputra delta is not included, so the rise people feel on the coast is larger."

(Synthetic expected result: Bay of Bengal faster than global by about 0.54 mm/yr, p = 0.006.)

## 2. Greenland vs Antarctica: same planet, different mechanism

Clicks: Variable **Ice mass** -> Region **Greenland** -> **Run analysis**; then Region **Antarctica** -> **Run analysis**. Optional: Compare **More variables** -> **Summer air temperature** on each.

Say: "Both ice sheets are losing mass, but not in the same way. Published studies show Greenland loses ice through summer surface melt and glaciers pouring ice into the sea. Antarctica loses ice mainly where warm ocean water thins West Antarctic glaciers; most of its surface stays below freezing. If two lines move together on this chart, that alone does not show one causes the other."

Sources: [IMBIE Team 2020, Greenland](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1855-2); [IMBIE Team 2018, Antarctica](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0179-y).

## 3. "Cannot tell yet": a short time range

Clicks: Variable **Summer air temperature** -> Region **Antarctica** -> drag the range handles to about 10 years (for example 2014-2023) -> **Run analysis**.

Say: "With only ten years, the app says it cannot tell yet. That does not mean nothing is changing. Year-to-year swings are large compared with the trend, so a real trend could be missed. The app estimates how many more years of data it would take to detect it."

(Check the warning "With only N years of data, a real trend could still be missed" appears.)
